# Credits and sources

TORRELD's training packs describe widely-practiced techniques and lean on
published motor-learning research. The **methods** are not owned by this project
- they are ideas and procedures, credited here to the people and work they come
from. TORRELD's own contribution is the packaging: the diagnosis framing, the
par-time drill structure, the written expression, and the mapping to research.
That expression is licensed CC BY-SA 4.0 (see [LICENSE-CONTENT.md](LICENSE-CONTENT.md));
the code is MIT (see [LICENSE](LICENSE)).

Each pack also carries its own **References** section in the app, with per-source
notes and links. This file is the consolidated list.

## Practitioners

- **Practical Shooting Training Group** - Ben Stoeger, Hwansik Kim, Joel Park.
  Par-time dry-fire structure, grip/index/reload framing, stage planning.
  https://www.practicalshootingtraininggroup.com
- **Steve Anderson** - *Refinement and Repetition* (the par-time dry-fire format
  this framework mirrors; several drills, including "Reload + 1", are re-expressed
  from its named drills) and the *That Shooting Show* podcast.
  https://www.andersonshooting.com
- **Modern Samurai Project** - Scott Jedlinski. Red-dot presentation: the draw
  delivers the dot to the eye. https://www.modernsamuraiproject.com
- **Brian Enos** - *Practical Shooting: Beyond Fundamentals* and the community
  forum. Visualization and being "in the moment" on stage. https://www.brianenos.com
- **Lanny Bassham** - *With Winning in Mind*. Mental-management lineage behind the
  match-mode mindset and rehearsal-as-practice.
- **Charlie Delta Academy** - plain-language grip and support-hand reference
  points. https://charliedeltaacademy.com

## Research

- Fitts (1954), *The information capacity of the human motor system*.
- Chase & Simon (1973), *Perception in chess* (chunking / expert memory).
- Vickers (1996), visual control when aiming at a far target (the "quiet eye").
- Masters (1992), *Knowledge, knerves and know-how* (re-investment).
- Holmes & Collins (2001), the PETTLEP model of motor imagery.
- Wulf (2013), attentional focus and motor learning (15-year review).
- Schmidt & Lee, *Motor Learning and Performance* (textbook).
- Meta-analyses and reviews on contextual interference, mental practice, and
  specificity/variability of practice (linked per pack in-app).
